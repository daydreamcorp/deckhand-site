In short

Deckhand has no account system. Your deck layout and pairing information stay on your phone and your paired computer. AI page generation is the one feature that leaves them: it goes through a small Deckhand-run service, and only if you turn it on. The outside parties involved are your app store (for purchases), that service and the AI providers behind it (OpenRouter and DeepSeek), Google (for Firebase analytics and crash reporting, for ads on the free tier, and for OS-level speech recognition if you use voice triggers), and your phone's own location service if you use geofenced pages.

Nothing below changes that — this document exists to describe the handful of places data leaves your device, in full, not to hedge it.

1

What Deckhand stores, and where

  • Deck layout (pages, buttons, dials, skins, bound actions) — a local file on your phone. There is no cloud sync or cloud backup feature today. The app does keep local safety copies of that file before risky operations like a schema migration, but those copies never leave the device.
  • Pairing records (which computers you've paired with, and their public identity key — never a secret) — stored locally on your phone.
  • Geofence profiles (name, coordinates, radius, which page to switch to) — stored locally on your phone. Never transmitted to us, to the paired computer, or to any third party.
  • Device identity key — a cryptographic keypair generated on your phone the first time you pair a computer, proving it's really your phone reconnecting later. Stays on your phone.
2

What crosses the network, and to whom

To your paired computer, on your local network only: your deck layout and button presses. The connection is end-to-end encrypted at the application layer — X25519 key exchange, AES-GCM, both devices' identities authenticated with Ed25519 signatures — independent of whether the underlying local-network transport itself uses TLS. This never goes through the internet or through any Deckhand-operated server; we don't operate one.

To your app store (Google Play / Apple App Store): purchase transactions for subscriptions and add-ons. We never see your payment details — the store handles that directly.

To Google (free tier only): standard AdMob advertising requests, which include an advertising identifier subject to your OS-level ad-tracking settings, to show the banner ad and rewarded-ad offers. This stops entirely — no requests at all — once you're on a paid tier.

To Google or Apple's speech recognition service (only if you use a voice-activated button): recognizing what you said may be processed on-device or by the platform's own cloud speech service, depending on your phone's settings — an OS-level behavior we don't control or see the result of beyond the recognized text, which stays on your phone to compare against your configured phrase. We never store or transmit your voice recording ourselves.

To Google/Apple's location services (only if you use geofenced pages): your phone's OS resolves your location; Deckhand only ever sees a latitude/longitude pair it uses locally, never sent anywhere by us.

3

What we collect via Firebase

Deckhand uses Firebase, a Google product, for five things: Analytics (product usage — which screens and features you use, pairing and subscription events, your subscription tier), Crashlytics (crash and error reports, including device model, OS version, and the state of the app when it crashed), Performance Monitoring (automatic app-start, network-request and screen-render timing — not the content of what you did, only how long it took), Remote Config (lets us tune a couple of app behaviors, such as the free-tier rewarded-ad cadence, without shipping a new build), and Cloud Messaging (push notification infrastructure — captures a device token so a future update could send you a notification; as of this build, none are sent).

None of this includes your deck layout, pairing records, geofence profiles, or anything else described under What Deckhand stores, and where — those stay exactly as private as they always have.

  • Region-dependent by default, always adjustable — outside the EEA and UK this collection starts automatically, the way most apps work. Inside the EEA and UK, Google's User Messaging Platform shows a consent prompt first and collection only starts if you agree. Regardless of region, Settings → Privacy has a toggle to turn analytics off at any time, and a Reset analytics ID action that starts you over with a fresh, unlinked pseudonymous identifier — the practical equivalent of deleting your analytics history, since there is no account for a deletion request to attach to.
  • Retention — analytics data is kept for 2 months from collection, then automatically deleted from Firebase's systems. That is the shorter of the two retention windows Firebase offers, chosen deliberately to keep only what is useful for recent troubleshooting and trends.
  • Where it goes — to Google, as the processor operating Firebase on our behalf, under Google's Standard Contractual Clauses for any transfer outside your region. Google does not use this data to build its own advertising profile of you; Firebase Analytics data is walled off from Google Ads targeting unless a developer explicitly links the two, which we don't.
  • Pseudonymous, not identified — none of this is tied to your name, email, or any account, because there isn't one. It is linked to a random identifier Firebase generates per install, which the Reset analytics ID action above lets you rotate at will.
4

AI page generation

This is off until you turn it on. Deckhand can build a deck page from a sentence you type. Doing that means sending something to a company outside Deckhand, so nothing is sent until you have read what goes and said yes. Turning it back off stops it again.

Who receives it. First a small service Deckhand runs itself, which holds the AI account and forwards the request; then OpenRouter, which routes it to DeepSeek's deepseek-v4-flash-0731 model. Every request is sent with routing that asks providers not to retain or train on it. Reaching that service from the public internet may involve a network provider that relays the connection; it carries the request the same way any internet hop does.

The service keeps a count of how many pages each installation has generated, so a daily limit can be applied. That count is a number and a timestamp against your computer's public pairing fingerprint — not your prompt, not the page, and nothing that names you.

What is sent. Only these:

  • The description you typed.
  • The size of the page being filled — its rows and columns.
  • Your computer's operating system, because a keyboard shortcut for the wrong one simply does nothing.
  • If OBS is connected, the names of its scenes.
  • When you ask to extend or revise an existing page: the labels of the tiles already on it and what type of thing each one does — "a hotkey", "a web request" — and nothing more.

What is never sent. What your buttons actually do. Not the commands, not the addresses, not the text you have saved, not the contents of any header — a button can hold a password you typed or a token for a service you use, and those are stripped before the request is built rather than filtered out of it afterwards. Also never sent: your computer's name, your phone's name, or the name of your deck.

Your phone does not make this call, and neither of your devices holds an AI account. Your phone asks the computer you have paired with; that computer asks Deckhand's service; the service holds the account and pays for the request. There is no key for you to supply and none stored on either device. Generation also does nothing on a computer with no phone paired to it — there would be no deck to build for.

What Deckhand does not do

  • No account system, no sign-up, no password.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with third parties for their own purposes.
  • No advertising once you're on a paid tier. The Firebase collection described above is separate from advertising and continues on paid tiers unless you turn it off in Settings → Privacy.
  • No collection of your deck layout, pairing records, or geofence profiles — by Firebase or anything else.
7

Your rights

Because Deckhand has no account system, there is no Deckhand-held profile of you to request, correct, or delete. Almost everything above either stays on your device or is handled by Google/Apple directly under their own privacy terms. The one exception is AI page generation, and only if you turned it on: Deckhand's own service keeps a count of pages generated per installation, against your computer's public pairing fingerprint, so a daily limit can be applied. Ask and it is deleted; it is a row of counters, and losing it costs you nothing but that day's limit resetting.

  • Analytics and crash reporting — turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy, and use Reset analytics ID for the erasure mechanism described under What we collect via Firebase. Since the data is pseudonymous and tied to no account, rotating that identifier is what a deletion request would otherwise achieve.
  • Ad personalization — controlled at the OS level (Android Settings → Privacy → Ads; iOS Settings → Privacy → Tracking), not by us.
  • Location, camera, microphone and notification permissions — revocable any time in your device's app-permission settings; the corresponding features simply stop working until re-granted, or for notifications nothing changes, since none are sent yet.
  • Purchase records — held by your app store; requests about them go to Google Play or the App Store directly.

If this section doesn't cover a request you have, use the contact information below and we'll do our best to help even where we hold no data ourselves.

8

Children's privacy

Deckhand is a utility for controlling your own computer and isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from them. This section carried more weight when the app collected nothing at all; now that analytics and crash reporting ship (see What we collect via Firebase), a real legal review should confirm both this section and the app's actual Firebase configuration — Google's child-directed-treatment settings in particular — are sufficient before store submission. COPPA and GDPR-K requirements vary by jurisdiction and this draft doesn't attempt to satisfy them on its own.
