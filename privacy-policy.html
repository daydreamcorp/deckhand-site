In short

Deckhand has no server and no account system. Your deck layout and pairing information stay on your phone and your paired computer. The only outside parties involved are your app store (for purchases), Google (for ads on the free tier, and for OS-level speech recognition if you use voice triggers), and your phone's own location service if you use geofenced pages.

Nothing below changes that — this document exists to describe the handful of places data leaves your device, in full, not to hedge it.

1

What Deckhand stores, and where

  • Deck layout (pages, buttons, dials, skins, bound actions) — a local file on your phone. There is no cloud sync or cloud backup feature today. The app does keep local safety copies of that file before risky operations like a schema migration, but those copies never leave the device.
  • Pairing records (which computers you've paired with, and their public identity key — never a secret) — stored locally on your phone.
  • Geofence profiles (name, coordinates, radius, which page to switch to) — stored locally on your phone. Never transmitted to us, to the paired computer, or to any third party.
  • Device identity key — a cryptographic keypair generated on your phone the first time you pair a computer, proving it's really your phone reconnecting later. Stays on your phone.
2

What crosses the network, and to whom

To your paired computer, on your local network only: your deck layout and button presses. The connection is end-to-end encrypted at the application layer — X25519 key exchange, AES-GCM, both devices' identities authenticated with Ed25519 signatures — independent of whether the underlying local-network transport itself uses TLS. This never goes through the internet or through any Deckhand-operated server; we don't operate one.

To your app store (Google Play / Apple App Store): purchase transactions for subscriptions and add-ons. We never see your payment details — the store handles that directly.

To Google (free tier only): standard AdMob advertising requests, which include an advertising identifier subject to your OS-level ad-tracking settings, to show the banner ad and rewarded-ad offers. This stops entirely — no requests at all — once you're on a paid tier.

To Google or Apple's speech recognition service (only if you use a voice-activated button): recognizing what you said may be processed on-device or by the platform's own cloud speech service, depending on your phone's settings — an OS-level behavior we don't control or see the result of beyond the recognized text, which stays on your phone to compare against your configured phrase. We never store or transmit your voice recording ourselves.

To Google/Apple's location services (only if you use geofenced pages): your phone's OS resolves your location; Deckhand only ever sees a latitude/longitude pair it uses locally, never sent anywhere by us.

What Deckhand does not do

  • No account system, no sign-up, no password.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with third parties for their own purposes.
  • No advertising or tracking of any kind once you're on a paid tier.
5

Your rights

Because Deckhand has no server and no account system, there is no Deckhand-held profile of you to request, correct, or delete — everything above either stays on your device or is handled by Google/Apple directly under their own privacy terms.

  • Ad personalization — controlled at the OS level (Android Settings → Privacy → Ads; iOS Settings → Privacy → Tracking), not by us.
  • Location, camera, and microphone permissions — revocable any time in your device's app-permission settings; the corresponding features simply stop working until re-granted.
  • Purchase records — held by your app store; requests about them go to Google Play or the App Store directly.

If this section doesn't cover a request you have, use the contact information below and we'll do our best to help even where we hold no data ourselves.

6

Children's privacy

Deckhand is a utility for controlling your own computer and isn't directed at children. We don't knowingly collect data from children; since we don't collect data from anyone beyond what's described above, there's nothing additional to say here — but formal COPPA/GDPR-K compliance requirements vary by jurisdiction and this draft doesn't attempt to satisfy them on its own.
