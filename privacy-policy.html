In short

Deckhand has no general account system. Your deck layout and pairing information stay on your phone and your paired computer. In configured closed-test builds, a one-time tester name is linked by the private Admin service to the phone's install ID, its usage telemetry (including whether you are using the app right now), its error reports and error screenshots, and paired-computer summary telemetry; it is not sent to Firebase. The name prompt is mandatory in the closed test: the app does not open past it until the service has confirmed the name. Two features send something built from them to a small Deckhand-run service: AI page generation (§4), off until you turn it on, and the desktop app's suggestion notifications (§5), on by default and switched off in Desk's Settings. During the closed test, the phone app and the desktop app also send usage and crash reports — the desktop app to that same service (§3), with a switch in Desk's Settings. None of them include what your buttons do, with one exception: a closed-test error screenshot (§3) shows whatever the app window displayed at that moment. The outside parties involved are your app store (for purchases), that service and the AI providers behind it (OpenRouter and DeepSeek), Google (for Firebase analytics and crash reporting, for ads on the free tier, and for OS-level speech recognition if you use voice triggers), and your phone's own location service if you use geofenced pages.

Nothing below changes that — this document exists to describe the handful of places data leaves your device, in full, not to hedge it.

1

What Deckhand stores, and where

  • Deck layout (pages, buttons, dials, skins, bound actions) — a local file on your phone. There is no cloud sync or cloud backup feature today. The app does keep local safety copies of that file before risky operations like a schema migration, but those copies never leave the device.
  • Pairing records (which computers you've paired with, and their public identity key — never a secret) — stored locally on your phone.
  • Geofence profiles (name, coordinates, radius, which page to switch to) — stored locally on your phone. Never transmitted to us, to the paired computer, or to any third party.
  • Device identity key — a cryptographic keypair generated on your phone the first time you pair a computer, proving it's really your phone reconnecting later. Stays on your phone.
  • Tester name (configured closed-test builds only) — entered in a launch modal, saved by the private Admin service against the phone's install ID, and cached in app-private storage (a file called tester_name.txt) after the service confirms it. Entering it is mandatory in the closed test: the prompt cannot be dismissed and the app does not open past it until the service has confirmed the name. The app checks the service first and does not ask again once that install has a saved name. Android's Auto Backup is not told to skip that file, so Android may include it in your phone's Google backup and restore it after a reinstall or onto a new phone, where it only fills in the name box; the service still has to confirm the name.
2

What crosses the network, and to whom

To your paired computer, on your local network only: your deck layout and button presses. The connection is end-to-end encrypted at the application layer — X25519 key exchange, AES-GCM, both devices' identities authenticated with Ed25519 signatures — independent of whether the underlying local-network transport itself uses TLS. This never goes through the internet or through any Deckhand-operated server.

To Deckhand's own service: AI page generation if you turn it on (§4), suggestion notifications (§5), and, during the closed test, usage and crash reports from the phone app and the desktop app (§3). Configured tester builds also send the name entered in the boot prompt, associated with the mobile install ID, and the error reports and screenshots described in §3. The name is not sent to Firebase. The service runs on our own hardware and is reachable only over an encrypted connection.

To your app store (Google Play / Apple App Store): purchase transactions for subscriptions and add-ons. We never see your payment details — the store handles that directly.

To Google (free tier only): standard AdMob advertising requests, which include an advertising identifier subject to your OS-level ad-tracking settings, to show the banner ad and rewarded-ad offers. This stops entirely — no requests at all — once you're on a paid tier.

To Google or Apple's speech recognition service (only if you use a voice-activated button): recognizing what you said may be processed on-device or by the platform's own cloud speech service, depending on your phone's settings — an OS-level behavior we don't control or see the result of beyond the recognized text, which stays on your phone to compare against your configured phrase. We never store or transmit your voice recording ourselves.

To Google/Apple's location services (only if you use geofenced pages): your phone's OS resolves your location; Deckhand only ever sees a latitude/longitude pair it uses locally, never sent anywhere by us.

3

What we collect via Firebase

Deckhand uses Firebase, a Google product, for five things: Analytics (product usage — which screens and features you use, pairing and subscription events, your subscription tier), Crashlytics (crash and error reports, including device model, OS version, and the state of the app when it crashed), Performance Monitoring (automatic app-start, network-request and screen-render timing — not the content of what you did, only how long it took), Remote Config (lets us tune a couple of app behaviors, such as the free-tier rewarded-ad cadence, without shipping a new build), and Cloud Messaging (push notification infrastructure — captures a device token so a future update could send you a notification; as of this build, none are sent).

None of this includes your deck layout, pairing records, geofence profiles, or anything else described under What Deckhand stores, and where — those stay exactly as private as they always have. (The closed-test error screenshots described below go only to Deckhand's own service, never to Firebase.)

  • During the closed test, analytics is always on outside the EEA and UK. Deckhand is in a closed testing phase, and test builds of the phone app collect usage and crash data from the start, without an off switch — the tester group is small enough that a few opt-outs would leave the results unreadable. The Settings → Privacy toggle is hidden in these builds because it would not be honoured. This is temporary: when Deckhand leaves the closed test, the toggle returns for everyone and this paragraph goes with it.
  • Inside the EEA and UK, nothing is forced. Google's User Messaging Platform shows a consent prompt first, collection only starts if you agree, and the Settings → Privacy toggle stays visible so you can withdraw at any time. A refusal is never overridden by a test build.
  • What the test collects: which screens you open and for how long, which controls you touch and how, which action kinds you fire and whether they succeed, deck and page counts, permission outcomes, connection state and latency, onboarding progress, and non-fatal errors. The tester name is sent separately to Admin; the Firebase event stream does not include it or the contents of anything, such as your shell commands, macro steps, typed text, clipboard, button or deck names, host names, IP addresses, Wi-Fi networks or geofence coordinates, and not which tile you touched. This describes the usage events: error screenshots are the exception, see Error reports and screenshots below.
  • Where it goes during the closed test: test builds also send this same event stream to the small private service we run ourselves, so that we can read it. Besides this event stream it receives only the tester identity and the error reports and screenshots described in the next bullets; it stores them on our own hardware, is reachable only over an encrypted connection, and is not shared with anyone. The event stream, the error reports and the screenshots are deleted when the closed test ends (tester identity records are the exception, see below).
  • Tester identity in the private Admin service: configured tester builds ask once for a name and send it separately from analytics consent. The prompt is mandatory in the closed test: it cannot be dismissed, it appears in every region whether or not you accepted analytics, and the app does not open past it until the service has confirmed the name, so it stays shut if the service cannot be reached. The app re-checks with the service each time it opens and about once a minute while open, so if an operator clears your name or blocks your installation the prompt returns or the app stays locked. The service associates the name with that phone's install ID and records that the name is held by that one installation, so the same name cannot be used on a second one; an operator can clear the stored name, lock it against changes, or block the installation. Admin shows the name with the phone's telemetry, live presence, error reports and screenshots, and the paired-computer summary forwarded by that phone. The name is not part of the Firebase event stream. The current Admin store does not expire identity rows automatically and they are not covered by the closed-test deletion above; the name and its one-installation record remain until an administrator removes the service data. Contact us to request access, correction, or removal.
  • Live presence (closed test): when the app comes to the foreground or goes to the background, when you open a screen and when you switch deck page, the phone sends that event to the private service straight away instead of waiting for the usual batch. This lets the person running the test see in Admin who is using the app right now, shown under the tester name, with the screen they are on, the page number and page count (never the page's name) and whether the app is in the foreground. It is part of the analytics event stream and follows the same collection rules as above: always on outside the EEA and UK during the closed test, consent first inside it.
  • Error reports and screenshots (closed test): when the phone app catches an error, a configured test build sends a report to the private service: the error's category, the name of the screen you were on, the deck page number and count, the app version, the install ID and a stack trace (code locations only, no error message). During the closed test only, it also sends a screenshot of Deckhand's own window as it looked when the error happened. A screenshot shows whatever the window shows, so it can contain deck and button names, host names, text you have typed into a visible field and the pairing screen; it is the exception to the lists of what is not collected above and in §7. It never captures another app or the rest of the device screen, is made in memory rather than saved to your phone's media library, and goes only to our own service, never to Firebase. The phone sends these under the same collection rule as the analytics stream (always on outside the EEA and UK during the closed test; inside it, only if you agreed); Desk's own screenshots follow Desk's switch, below. Admin shows each report and screenshot under the tester name of the install that sent it. Retention: screenshots are not deleted on a timer; the service keeps at most 200 images or 500 MB, whichever is reached first, and removes the oldest as new ones arrive. Error reports and screenshots are deleted when the closed test ends, and on request (see Your rights).
  • Retention — analytics data is kept for 2 months from collection, then automatically deleted from Firebase's systems. That is the shorter of the two retention windows Firebase offers, chosen deliberately to keep only what is useful for recent troubleshooting and trends.
  • Where it goes — to Google, as the processor operating Firebase on our behalf, under Google's Standard Contractual Clauses for any transfer outside your region. Google does not use this data to build its own advertising profile of you; Firebase Analytics data is walled off from Google Ads targeting unless a developer explicitly links the two, which we don't.
  • Firebase remains pseudonymous — Firebase analytics is not tied to the tester name entered for Deckhand Admin, an email, or an account. It uses a random identifier generated per install. The separate name-to-phone association exists only in Deckhand's private Admin service.

The desktop app's own reports

The desktop app — Deckhand Host, Deckhand Desk and the installer — reports on itself directly to Deckhand's own service. It does not use Firebase or any other third-party SDK. Before this, a desktop app with no phone paired, or one that crashed, left nothing but a log file on your computer.

  • What it sends. Usage: when it starts and stops and why, whether an update installed, whether pairing a phone worked (and if not, what kind of failure — never the PIN), how long phones stay connected, every 15 minutes how many actions ran and failed by kind and how long they took, its own CPU and memory use, which parts of Desk's window you used, how AI page generations ended and how long they took (never what you typed). Errors: warning and error lines from its own log, grouped by where in the code they came from, with a count. Crashes: when a part of the app stops unexpectedly, where in the code it happened, a stack trace, and the last few hundred lines of its log; and, when the app was stopped without shutting down, that it happened and whether the computer had been restarted. Each report says which of the three programs sent it, its version, your operating system and version, processor type and language.
  • What is removed first. Before anything is saved for sending, every piece of free text is stripped of your user and computer names, home folder, IP and MAC addresses, e-mail addresses, and anything shaped like a key, token or device ID. Button contents, commands, typed text and deck names are never part of a report in the first place, with one exception. When reporting is enabled, Desk also captures its visible app window for a new error group (including a Host error, if Host asks while Desk is visible). That image can show whatever Desk is displaying — deck and button names, host names, text in visible fields, the pairing screen — and text scrubbing does not alter pixels. Captures stay inside Desk's visible app window, are tied to the same random install ID rather than to a name, and share the capacity limit and end-of-test deletion described under Error reports and screenshots.
  • How it is identified. By a random install ID created on your computer, which is not derived from and cannot be linked to the key your computer uses to pair with your phone. The service does not store the address your reports came from.
  • Your choice. During the closed test this is on by default, in every region. Desk → Settings → Privacy → Send usage and crash reports turns it off at any time, and turning it off deletes every report still waiting on your computer, so nothing collected before that moment is sent afterwards. Setting the environment variable DECKHAND_TELEMETRY=0 turns it off regardless of the switch.
  • Where it goes and for how long. To the same private service described above, on our own hardware, over an encrypted connection, shared with no one, and deleted when the closed test ends.
4

AI page generation

This is off until you turn it on. Deckhand can build a deck page from a sentence you type. Doing that means sending something to a company outside Deckhand, so nothing is sent until you have read what goes and said yes. Turning it back off stops it again.

Who receives it. First a small service Deckhand runs itself, which holds the AI account and forwards the request; then OpenRouter, which routes it to DeepSeek's deepseek-v4.1-flash model. Every request is sent with routing that asks providers not to retain or train on it. Reaching that service from the public internet may involve a network provider that relays the connection; it carries the request the same way any internet hop does.

The service keeps a count of how many pages each installation has generated, so a daily limit can be applied. Separately, when you agree to this AI consent, the description is retained on Deckhand's Pi in AES-256-GCM encrypted form for automated AI Studio improvements. The key remains on that Pi; the prompt is not included in analytics or exposed through a human-readable dashboard.

What is sent. Only these:

  • The description you typed.
  • The size of the page being filled — its rows and columns.
  • Your computer's operating system, because a keyboard shortcut for the wrong one simply does nothing.
  • If OBS is connected, the names of its scenes.
  • When you ask to extend or revise an existing page: the labels of the tiles already on it and what type of thing each one does — "a hotkey", "a web request" — and nothing more.

What is never sent. What your buttons actually do. Not the commands, not the addresses, not the text you have saved, not the contents of any header — a button can hold a password you typed or a token for a service you use, and those are stripped before the request is built rather than filtered out of it afterwards. Also never sent: your computer's name, your phone's name, or the name of your deck.

Neither of your devices holds an AI account. Your phone asks the computer you have paired with; that computer asks Deckhand's service; the service holds the account and pays for the request. The phone additionally uploads a consented retention copy to Deckhand's Pi, but holds neither an AI credential nor the encryption key. Generation also does nothing on a computer with no phone paired to it — there would be no deck to build for.

5

Suggestion notifications

This is on by default. Deckhand's desktop app (Host and Desk) can notice how you actually use your deck and, up to four times a day, show a short notification suggesting something to try next — clicking it opens AI Studio with that suggestion already typed in. Desk's Settings has a switch for this; turning it off stops both the notifications and the once-a-day request that generates them.

Who receives it. The same small Deckhand-run service AI page generation uses, and from there OpenRouter and the same underlying model — no new outside party.

What is sent, once a day. A plain-text summary built on your paired computer, not your phone:

  • The names of the pages in your deck, and how full each one is — a count, not its contents.
  • Which kinds of action and control you've used, and how often — "hotkey", "OBS", "a bounded dial" — as counts, not what any specific one does.
  • Whether you've opened AI Studio before.
  • Whether you've changed your deck's skin from the default.

What is never sent. The same rule AI page generation follows: what your buttons actually do. Not commands, not addresses, not saved text, not header contents. Also never sent: your computer's name, your phone's name, or the name of your deck.

This local usage summary is kept only on your paired computer. It is separate from the Firebase analytics described in What we collect via Firebase and is read only to build the daily request above — never uploaded anywhere on its own.

What Deckhand does not do

  • No account system, no sign-up, no password.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with third parties for their own purposes.
  • No advertising once you're on a paid tier. The Firebase collection described above is separate from advertising and continues on paid tiers unless you turn it off in Settings → Privacy.
  • No collection of your deck layout's contents — what your buttons do, their bound actions, pairing records, or geofence profiles — by Firebase or anything else. There are two deliberate, narrow exceptions to "layout". Suggestion notifications (§5) send page names and coarse per-kind usage counts, never button contents, and you can turn them off. And, during the closed test, an error screenshot is a picture of the app window, so it can show anything on screen at that moment, including deck and button names, host names, typed text and the pairing screen; it goes only to our own service, never to Firebase, and is kept under the capacity limit and end-of-test deletion described under Error reports and screenshots.

Your rights

Deckhand has no general account system. During the closed test, Deckhand's private Admin service does hold the tester name you enter, associated with your phone's install ID, alongside that install's telemetry (including live presence), error reports and error screenshots; it also holds the desktop app's own reports and screenshots, which carry a random install ID rather than a name, so they can be found only through that ID. Contact us using the information below (admin@daydreamcorp.com) to request access, correction, or deletion of any of it, and tell us the tester name you entered so we can find your phone's records; an administrator then removes them from the service. Without a request, screenshots go only when the capacity limit pushes them out or the closed test ends, and tester identity records are not deleted automatically. Other data described above either stays on your device or is handled by Google/Apple directly under their own privacy terms. The one remaining exception is AI page generation, and only if you turned it on: Deckhand's own service keeps a count of pages generated per installation, against your computer's public pairing fingerprint, so a daily limit can be applied. The same consent retains the AI Studio description encrypted for automated product improvements; it is not a readable dashboard record. Ask and the service deletes the retained data associated with that installation.

  • Analytics and crash reporting — on the phone, turn it off in Settings → Privacy wherever that toggle is shown (see What we collect via Firebase for where it is during the closed test); on the computer, in Desk → Settings → Privacy. This stops the analytics and crash-reporting streams, and the phone's automatic error reports and screenshots; it does not control the separate tester-name record described above, which is mandatory in the closed test. Firebase analytics remains pseudonymous, and Firebase's 2-month retention then ages out what it already collected. Where the toggle is hidden (outside the EEA and UK during the closed test), uninstalling the test build or leaving the tester programme is what stops collection, and it does not delete what our own service already holds. Contact us to request removal of data held by the private Admin service.
  • Ad personalization — controlled at the OS level (Android Settings → Privacy → Ads; iOS Settings → Privacy → Tracking), not by us.
  • Location, camera, microphone and notification permissions — revocable any time in your device's app-permission settings; the corresponding features simply stop working until re-granted, or for notifications nothing changes, since none are sent yet.
  • Purchase records — held by your app store; requests about them go to Google Play or the App Store directly.

If this section doesn't cover a request you have, use the contact information below and we'll do our best to help even where we hold no data ourselves.

Children's privacy

Deckhand is a utility for controlling your own computer and isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from them. This section carried more weight when the app collected nothing at all; now that analytics and crash reporting ship (see What we collect via Firebase), a real legal review should confirm both this section and the app's actual Firebase configuration — Google's child-directed-treatment settings in particular — are sufficient before store submission. COPPA and GDPR-K requirements vary by jurisdiction and this draft doesn't attempt to satisfy them on its own.

The tester community on Discord

This is separate from the app. It applies only to people who join Deckhand's tester Discord server. Installing or using Deckhand does not put you there, and nothing in the app depends on it. Discord itself hosts the server and handles your account under its own privacy policy; what follows is what Day Dream Corp does with what you write there.

What is saved. The messages you write in the server's channels and threads, with your Discord user ID and names, the channel they were written in, when, and any image or text file you attach (other kinds of file are recorded by name only). Not saved: direct messages, other bots' messages, and the server's admin-log channel. Saving is automatic for everything written in the server, filed per channel. When it was introduced, the previous 60 days of messages were saved too; anything older was not.

Why, and who sees it. To find bugs and improvements to make to Deckhand from what testers tell us. The messages are stored on Deckhand's own Raspberry Pi, readable only by the service's own account and its administrator, and read once a day by an automated AI assistant, Claude, made by Anthropic. That means the text you wrote is sent to Anthropic while the assistant reads it. The assistant treats what you write as a description of a problem, never as an instruction.

How long. Once a message has been read and used, it is deleted from the Pi and from the computer the assistant runs on. A message that has not been read is deleted after 60 days regardless. When a message helps change Deckhand, we keep a private log entry — your Discord name, a short quote of the part that helped, and what changed — so the people who helped can be thanked. That log is kept until you ask us to remove your entries.

Your control. In the server, /message-collection opt-out deletes every message saved from you and stops it from continuing; /message-collection status shows what is held; deleting one of your messages in Discord deletes our copy; editing it replaces ours. Do not post passwords, pairing PINs or keys: anything you post is saved. To have your name removed from the thanks log, or for any other request about this, contact us as in Contact.
