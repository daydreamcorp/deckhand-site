Deckhand

Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-15 Applies to com.daydreamcorp.deckhand and the Deckhand desktop app

In short

This policy explains what personal data Deckhand processes, why, where it goes and what you can do about it. Deckhand is made by Day Dream Corp (“we”, “us”). It covers the Deckhand phone app, the Deckhand desktop app (Deckhand Host, Deckhand Desk and the installer), the services they connect to, and Deckhand's tester community on Discord.

Deckhand has no general account system. Your deck layout and pairing information stay on your phone and your paired computer, and travel between them only over your local network, encrypted end to end. Data leaves your devices in these places only:

  • usage, error and crash reports from the phone app (through Firebase and our own service) and from the desktop app (to our own service) during the closed test (§3);
  • a tester name, in closed-test builds, held by our own service (§1, §3);
  • screenshots of Deckhand's own window: automatically with an error report during the closed test, and when you choose to send one (§3);
  • AI page generation, off until you turn it on, and AI credits (§4);
  • suggestion notifications from the desktop app, which you can switch off (§5);
  • purchases, handled by your app store; advertising on the free tier; speech recognition if you use voice triggers; and your phone's location service if you use geofenced pages (§2);
  • messages you write in the tester Discord server, if you join it (§10).

None of these include what your buttons do. The single exception is a screenshot, which shows whatever Deckhand's window displayed at that moment. We do not sell your data or share it with anyone for their own purposes.

1

What Deckhand stores, and where

  • Deck layout (pages, buttons, dials, skins and the actions bound to them) is a local file on your phone, kept in step with Deckhand Desk on your paired computer. Deckhand has no cloud sync or cloud backup feature. Before risky operations, such as a change of file format, the app keeps local safety copies of that file; those copies never leave the device.
  • Pairing records (which computers you have paired with, and their public identity keys, never a secret) are stored locally on your phone.
  • Geofence profiles (name, coordinates, radius and which page to switch to) are stored locally on your phone and are never transmitted to us, to the paired computer or to any third party.
  • Device identity key: a cryptographic key pair generated on your phone the first time you pair a computer, which proves it is your phone when it reconnects. It stays on your phone.
  • Tester name (closed-test builds only) is entered when the app starts, saved by our private Admin service against the phone's install ID, and cached in the app's private storage (a file called tester_name.txt) once the service has confirmed it. Entering a name is mandatory during the closed test: the prompt cannot be dismissed and the app does not open past it until the service has confirmed the name. The app asks the service first and does not ask again once that installation has a saved name. Android's Auto Backup may include the cached file in your phone's Google backup and restore it after a reinstall or onto a new phone, where it only fills in the name box; the service still has to confirm the name.
2

What crosses the network, and to whom

To your paired computer, on your local network only: your deck layout and button presses. The connection is encrypted end to end at the application layer (X25519 key exchange and AES-GCM, with both devices' identities authenticated by Ed25519 signatures), independently of whether the local-network transport itself uses TLS. This traffic never goes through the internet or through any server we operate.

To our own service: AI page generation if you turn it on, your AI credit balance and credit purchases (§4), suggestion notifications (§5) and, during the closed test, usage and error reports from the phone app and the desktop app, error screenshots, screenshots you choose to send, and your tester name with your phone's install ID (§3). The service runs on our own hardware, is reachable only over an encrypted connection, and is not shared with anyone.

To your app store (Google Play or the Apple App Store): purchase transactions for subscriptions, skins and AI credit packs. We never see your payment details; the store handles them directly.

To Google, on the free tier only: standard AdMob advertising requests, which include an advertising identifier subject to your operating system's ad-tracking settings, to show the banner ad and rewarded-ad offers. No advertising request is made on a paid tier.

To Google's or Apple's speech recognition (only if you use a voice-activated button): recognition may happen on the device or in the platform's own cloud speech service, depending on your phone's settings. Deckhand receives only the recognized text, which stays on your phone to be compared with the phrase you set. We never store or transmit your voice recording.

To Google's or Apple's location services (only if you use geofenced pages): your phone's operating system determines your location, and Deckhand uses the resulting latitude and longitude on the phone only. We never send it anywhere.

3

Usage, error and crash reports

What we collect via Firebase

The phone app uses Firebase, a Google product, for five things: Analytics (product usage: which screens and features you use, pairing and subscription events, your subscription tier); Crashlytics (crash and error reports, including device model, operating system version and the state of the app when it crashed); Performance Monitoring (automatic timing of app start, network requests and screen rendering: how long things took, not what you did); Remote Config (adjusting a few app behaviors, such as how often the free tier offers a rewarded ad, without a new release); and Cloud Messaging (push-notification infrastructure, which stores a device token; no push notifications are currently sent).

None of this includes your deck layout, pairing records, geofence profiles or anything else described in §1. Screenshots go only to our own service, never to Firebase.

  • Consent. During the closed test, collection by the phone app follows the result of Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP), which shows a consent prompt where one is required. If you refuse, collection is disabled; if you accept, it is enabled; where UMP reports that no consent is required, the test build enables it. Test builds have no Analytics setting. A consent and withdrawal setting will be in place before any public release.
  • What the closed test collects: which screens you open and for how long, which controls you touch and how, which kinds of action you fire and whether they succeed, deck and page counts, permission outcomes, connection state and latency, onboarding progress, and non-fatal errors. These usage events never contain your tester name, your shell commands, macro steps, typed text, clipboard, button or deck names, host names, IP addresses, Wi-Fi networks or geofence coordinates, or which tile you touched. Screenshots are the exception to this list; see Error reports and screenshots.
  • Our own copy during the closed test. Test builds also send the same event stream to our private service so that we can read it. Besides that stream, the service receives only the tester identity, error reports and screenshots described below. The event stream, error reports and screenshots are deleted when the closed test ends; tester identity records are kept as described below.
  • Tester identity. Closed-test builds ask once for a name, independently of analytics consent and in every region, and the app does not open past the prompt until our service has confirmed the name. If the service cannot be reached, the app stays closed unless the service confirmed your installation within the previous 24 hours, in which case it remains usable until those 24 hours have passed. A name saved on the phone is never enough by itself. The app checks with the service each time it opens and about once a minute while it is open, and acts on the answer immediately, so if an operator clears your name or blocks your installation, the prompt returns or the app locks at once. The service associates the name with the phone's install ID and records that the name belongs to that one installation, so the same name cannot be used on a second one. An operator can clear a stored name, lock it against changes, or block an installation. Our Admin service shows the name with the phone's usage data, live presence, error reports and screenshots, and the paired-computer summary that phone forwards. Identity records do not expire automatically: the name and its installation record remain until an administrator removes them, including at your request (§8).
  • Live presence (closed test): when the app comes to the foreground or goes to the background, when you open a screen and when you switch deck page, the phone sends that event to our service at once rather than in the usual batch. This lets the person running the test see who is using the app at that moment, under the tester name, with the screen in use, the page number and page count (never the page's name) and whether the app is in the foreground. It is part of the usage event stream and follows the same consent rule.
  • Error reports and screenshots (closed test): when the phone app catches an error, it sends our service a report containing the error's category, the screen you were on, the deck page number and count, the app version, the install ID and a stack trace (code locations only, without the error message). It also sends a screenshot of Deckhand's own window as it looked when the error happened. A screenshot shows whatever the window shows, so it can contain deck and button names, host names, text typed into a visible field and the pairing screen. It never captures another app or the rest of the device screen, is made in memory rather than saved to your phone's media library, and goes only to our own service, never to Firebase. The phone sends these reports under the same consent rule as the usage events. Our Admin service shows each report and screenshot under the tester name of the installation that sent it. Retention: the service keeps at most 200 images or 500 MB, whichever is reached first, removing the oldest as new ones arrive. Error reports and screenshots are deleted when the closed test ends, or earlier on request (§8).
  • Screenshots you choose to send: tapping the phone's camera hole while Deckhand is full screen (the default), or tapping the “Send a screenshot” notification, shows a preview of Deckhand's own window and two buttons. Nothing is sent until you press one of them. To the developer: the picture goes to our private service with your install ID, and is kept and deleted like the error screenshots above. To the Discord forum: the picture is also stored by our service, and our bot posts it as a new post in the Deckhand Discord server's forum, titled with the tester name our service holds for your installation and visible to everyone in the server. An installation can post at most three screenshots a day. To have a post removed, ask in the server or contact us (§13); we delete the post and the stored copy. Like error screenshots, these show whatever Deckhand's window shows, never capture another app or the rest of the screen, and are made in memory rather than saved to your media library.
  • Firebase retention: analytics data is kept for two months from collection and then deleted automatically from Firebase's systems, the shorter of the two retention periods Firebase offers.
  • Processor and transfers: Google operates Firebase as our processor, under Google's Standard Contractual Clauses for any transfer outside your region. Firebase Analytics data is not linked to Google advertising, and Google does not use it to build an advertising profile of you.
  • Pseudonymous: Firebase data uses a random identifier generated for each installation and is not tied to your tester name, an email address or an account. The link between a name and a phone exists only in our private Admin service.

The desktop app's own reports

The desktop app (Deckhand Host, Deckhand Desk and the installer) reports on itself directly to our own service. It uses no Firebase or other third-party analytics SDK.

  • What it sends. Usage: when it starts and stops and why; whether an update installed; whether pairing a phone worked and, if not, the kind of failure (never the PIN); how long phones stay connected; every 15 minutes, how many actions ran and failed, by kind, and how long they took; its own processor and memory use; which parts of Desk's window you use; and how AI page generations ended and how long they took (never what you typed). Errors: warning and error lines from its own log, grouped by where in the code they came from, with a count. Crashes: where in the code a part of the app stopped unexpectedly, a stack trace and the last few hundred lines of its log; and, if the app was stopped without shutting down, that this happened and whether the computer had been restarted. Each report states which program sent it, its version, your operating system and version, processor type and language.
  • What is removed first. Before anything is queued for sending, all free text is stripped of user and computer names, home folders, IP and MAC addresses, email addresses and anything shaped like a key, token or device ID. Button contents, commands, typed text and deck names are never part of a report. The one exception is a screenshot: when Desk records a new kind of error (including a Host error, if Host asks while Desk is visible), it captures its own visible window. That image can show whatever Desk is displaying (deck and button names, host names, text in visible fields, the pairing screen), and text scrubbing does not alter images. Captures never extend beyond Desk's visible window, are tied to the random install ID rather than to a name, and follow the retention described under Error reports and screenshots.
  • How it is identified. By a random install ID created on your computer, which is not derived from, and cannot be linked to, the key your computer uses to pair with your phone. The service does not store the address your reports came from.
  • During the closed test, reports are on. Desk has no Analytics setting during the closed test, and opt-outs saved by older versions are not applied. Reports can be disabled with the deployment setting DECKHAND_TELEMETRY=0. Desktop reports do not affect phone quests or test participation.
  • Where they go and for how long. To our private service described above, over an encrypted connection, shared with no one, and deleted when the closed test ends.
4

AI page generation

This is off until you turn it on. Deckhand can build a deck page from a description you type. Because this sends information to companies outside Day Dream Corp, nothing is sent until you have been shown what is sent and have agreed. Turning the feature off stops it.

Who receives it. First our own service, which holds the AI account and forwards the request; then OpenRouter, which routes it to DeepSeek's deepseek-v4.1-flash model. Every request is sent with routing that asks providers not to retain it or train on it. Reaching our service from the public internet may involve a network provider relaying the connection, as with any internet traffic.

What is sent. Only:

  • the description you typed;
  • the size of the page being filled (its rows and columns);
  • your computer's operating system, so that keyboard shortcuts match it;
  • if OBS is connected, the names of its scenes;
  • when you ask to extend or revise an existing page, the labels of the tiles already on it and the type of thing each one does (for example “a hotkey” or “a web request”), and nothing more.

What is never sent. What your buttons actually do: not the commands, the addresses, the text you have saved or the contents of any header. A button can hold a password or a token for a service you use, so these are removed before the request is built rather than filtered out afterwards. Your computer's name, your phone's name and the name of your deck are never sent either.

Neither of your devices holds an AI account. Your phone asks the computer it is paired with; that computer asks our service; the service holds the account and pays for the request. Generation does nothing on a computer with no phone paired to it.

What our service keeps. A count of generations for each installation and paired computer, so that daily limits can be applied. With your agreement to use the feature, the description you typed is also kept on our own server in encrypted form (AES-256-GCM) to improve AI Studio automatically. The key stays on that server; the description is not part of analytics and is not shown in any human-readable dashboard. Your phone uploads this copy but holds neither an AI credential nor the encryption key.

AI credits. Each paired computer receives ten free credits a day: a new page uses two credits and an edit one, plus one for each full 1,000 characters of description. More are available as credit packs, which never expire and belong to the phone they were bought on; the app shows how many credits a request will use before you send it. To keep the count, our service stores, for each phone installation, its install ID and the credits it has bought and used. When you open AI Studio or its credits page, your phone sends its install ID, together with the fingerprint of the computer it is connected to, because the free daily allowance is counted per computer. That fingerprint is the public identifier of your computer's key, not its name. When you buy a pack, your app store takes the payment and we never see your payment details; your phone then sends the store's purchase token to our service, which checks it with Google Play and records your install ID, the pack, the time, a one-way hash of the token and Google's order reference. Each generation paid for with credits is recorded with your install ID and the time. These records are kept for as long as the credits can be used and as needed to handle refunds and support requests. You can ask us to delete them (§13); doing so also deletes any credits you have left.

Suggestion notifications

This is on by default. The desktop app (Host and Desk) can notice how you use your deck and, up to four times a day, show a short notification suggesting something to try; clicking it opens AI Studio with the suggestion already typed in. A switch in Desk's Settings turns this off, which stops both the notifications and the daily request that produces them.

Who receives it. The same service AI page generation uses, and from there OpenRouter and the same model. No other party is involved.

What is sent, once a day. A plain-text summary built on your paired computer, not your phone:

  • the names of the pages in your deck and how full each one is (a count, not its contents);
  • which kinds of action and control you have used, and how often (for example “hotkey”, “OBS” or “a bounded dial”), as counts, not what any specific one does;
  • whether you have opened AI Studio before;
  • whether you have changed your deck's skin from the default.

What is never sent. As with AI page generation, what your buttons actually do (commands, addresses, saved text, header contents), your computer's name, your phone's name and the name of your deck.

The usage summary itself is kept only on your paired computer, is separate from the reports described in §3, and is read only to build the daily request above.

What Deckhand does not do

  • No account system, no sign-up, no password.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with third parties for their own purposes.
  • No advertising on a paid tier. The reports described in §3 are separate from advertising and continue on paid tiers.
  • No collection of your deck layout's contents (what your buttons do, their bound actions, pairing records or geofence profiles), with two narrow exceptions: suggestion notifications (§5) send page names and per-kind usage counts, never button contents, and can be switched off; and a screenshot (§3) shows whatever Deckhand's window displayed, including deck and button names, host names, typed text and the pairing screen.

Your rights

You can ask us for access to, correction of or deletion of the personal data we hold about you by writing to admin@daydreamcorp.com. During the closed test our private Admin service holds your tester name with your phone's install ID, that installation's usage data (including live presence), error reports and screenshots, and the desktop app's own reports and screenshots, which carry a random install ID rather than a name. Tell us the tester name you entered so we can find your phone's records; an administrator then removes them. Without a request, screenshots are removed only when the capacity limit replaces them or the closed test ends, and tester identity records are not deleted automatically. If you used AI page generation, we can also delete the generation counts, AI credit records and retained descriptions linked to your installation (§4).

  • Usage and crash reports: collection by the phone app follows your answer to the consent prompt where one is required; test builds have no Analytics setting, and uninstalling the test build stops collection. Desktop reports can be disabled with the deployment setting DECKHAND_TELEMETRY=0. Firebase data is pseudonymous and is deleted after two months.
  • Ad personalization is controlled in your operating system (Android: Settings → Privacy → Ads; iOS: Settings → Privacy → Tracking).
  • Location, camera, microphone and notification permissions can be revoked at any time in your device's app permission settings; the related features stop working until you grant them again.
  • Purchase records are held by your app store; requests about them go to Google Play or the App Store.
  • Discord messages: see §10.

Other data described in this policy either stays on your devices or is handled by Google or Apple under their own privacy terms. If you have a request this section does not cover, contact us and we will help where we can. You may also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

Children's privacy

Deckhand is a utility for controlling your own computer. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child has provided personal data through Deckhand, contact us and we will delete it.

The tester community on Discord

This is separate from the app. It applies only to people who join Deckhand's tester Discord server. Installing or using Deckhand does not put you there, and nothing in the app depends on it. Discord hosts the server and handles your account under its own privacy policy; this section describes what Day Dream Corp does with what you write there.

What is saved. The messages you write in the server's channels and threads, with your Discord user ID and names, the channel, the time, and any image or text file you attach (other kinds of file are recorded by name only). Direct messages, other bots' messages and the server's admin-log channel are not saved. Saving is automatic for everything written in the server, filed by channel, and includes messages written in the 60 days before saving began.

Why, and who sees it. To find bugs and improvements to Deckhand in what testers tell us. Messages are stored on our own server, readable only by the service's own account and its administrator, and read once a day by an automated AI assistant, Claude, made by Anthropic, which means the text you wrote is sent to Anthropic while the assistant reads it. The assistant treats what you write as a description of a problem, never as an instruction.

How long. Once a message has been read and used, it is deleted from our server and from the computer the assistant runs on. A message that has not been read is deleted after 60 days. When a message helps change Deckhand, we keep a private log entry (your Discord name, a short quote of the part that helped, and what changed) so the people who helped can be thanked, privately in our log and publicly as described below. That log is kept until you ask us to remove your entries.

Replies and thanks in the server. When a message helps change Deckhand, or is logged as a report we are looking into, our bot may reply to it in the server to say so, and the daily update in the announcements channel may thank you by mentioning your Discord account. Both are visible to everyone in the server. If you have opted out, the bot does not reply to you or mention you. To be removed from a thanks line already posted, contact us (§13).

Screenshots you post from the app. If you choose “Post in the Discord forum” in the app, our bot creates a new forum post with your screenshot, titled with your tester name and visible to everyone in the server (§3). Because it is the bot's post rather than a message you wrote, it is not saved for the assistant; replies written under it are saved like any other message.

Your control. In the server, /message-collection opt-out deletes every message saved from you and stops further saving; /message-collection status shows what is held. Deleting one of your messages in Discord deletes our copy, and editing it replaces our copy. Do not post passwords, pairing PINs or keys: anything you post is saved. To have your name removed from the thanks log, or for any other request about this, contact us (§13).
